## 0. Front Matter

### Title Page

**Dutton, Boyanowsky & Bond (2005)**

***Extreme Mass Homicide: From Military Massacre to Genocide***

An Interactive Digital Synopsis

### Executive Summary

Why do ordinary people kill their neighbors? Not the rare sadist, not the fanatic at the podium — the farmer, the clerk, the reservist, the teenager at the roadblock. Every case in this synopsis, from a six-week rampage in Nanking to a hundred days of machetes in Rwanda, ultimately turns on that question, and every comfortable answer to it fails.

Dutton, Boyanowsky, and Bond's 2005 paper confronts the question directly. Their central move is to treat events we usually keep in separate boxes — military massacres, genocides, politically driven mass killings — as members of a single family, **extreme mass homicide**, and to ask what mechanisms they share. Their answer is unsettling precisely because it is so well documented: the decisive variables are rarely found inside individual perpetrators. They are found in the *situations* that states, armies, and movements construct around them.

This synopsis distills that argument and tests it against history. In brief:

*   **A shared anatomy.** Massacre, genocide, and political slaughter differ in scale, intent, and duration — the paper's typology takes these differences seriously — yet they escalate through recognizably similar stages: grievance, propaganda, a decision to eliminate, organization, execution, denial.
*   **Situations over dispositions.** The psychological engine of mass killing is built from ordinary human machinery — obedience to authority, conformity, group polarization, moral disengagement — deliberately assembled and aimed. "Monsters" are not recruited; they are manufactured on site.
*   **Dehumanization as the license.** Before the killing comes the language: cockroaches, vermin, disease, traitors. Every case documented here invested heavily in redefining its victims as something other than human, because empathy must be dismantled before violence can be industrialized.
*   **Escalation is visible — and interruptible.** The process model at the heart of the paper doubles as an early-warning instrument. Each stage casts observable shadows: militias forming, target groups disarmed, elimination rhetoric entering official speech. At each stage, intervention is still possible.
*   **Stories carry the meaning.** Eight historical case studies pair narrative vignettes — grounded in the testimony of documented witnesses — with analytic reconstruction, because the pattern only becomes real when you can see the people inside it.

### A Note Before You Begin

This synopsis describes historical atrocities, including violence against children, in enough detail to be truthful. It does so as soberly as the material allows: nothing here is sensationalized, and nothing graphic is included for effect. The narrative passages stay close to documented testimony — named witnesses, dated diaries, tribunal records — because the victims of these events were repeatedly denied the dignity of being believed. Read at your own pace; the table of contents will always let you step back to the analytical sections.

### How to Use This Synopsis

This document speaks in two voices, and it tells you which one you are hearing. **Analytic sections** — marked with a cool blue edge — carry the argument: definitions, theory, comparative analysis, the process model. They are precise, sourced, and skeptical. **Narrative vignettes** — marked in warm gold — open each historical case at human scale: one morning, one village, one witness. Neither voice is decoration. The analysis keeps the stories honest; the stories keep the analysis from becoming abstraction.

You can read straight through, or navigate by need. If you want the conceptual framework, start with Part I and the process model in Part IV. If you teach or study specific events, each case study in Part II stands alone, with context, chronology, atrocity pattern, psychological drivers, and aftermath. Highlighted terms open the glossary — hover for a short definition, click for the full entry. The process-model diagram in Part IV is interactive: select any stage to see how it works and where it can be interrupted.
