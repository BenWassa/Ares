## 1. Scope & Purpose

In 2005, Donald G. Dutton, Ehor O. Boyanowsky and Michael H. Bond published *Extreme Mass Homicide: From Military Massacre to Genocide*. The paper places forms of large-scale intentional killing that are often studied separately into a comparative frame and asks what political, historical, organizational and psychological mechanisms they may share — and where their contexts and dynamics differ.

Ares is a digital synopsis and interpretive publication built around that paper. Its purpose is explanatory rather than predictive. The publication compares eight historical cases with the paper's mechanisms while preserving case-specific context, uncertainty and source status.

Three boundaries govern the reading:

* **Explanation is not excuse.** Situational and organizational explanations do not erase individual or institutional responsibility.
* **Comparison is not equivalence.** Genocide, political slaughter and military massacre differ in intent, duration, legal classification, historical setting and violence dynamics even when some mechanisms overlap.
* **Synthesis is not a universal ladder.** Part IV groups source-mapped material from Dutton et al. into four interacting domains. Those domains are an Ares synthesis, not necessary or sufficient stages, a severity scale, or a validated early-warning instrument.

The project therefore separates what the source paper argues, what historical case material reports, and what Ares synthesizes. Where the current legacy case corpus still lacks source-level tracing for a claim, estimate or quotation, the interface identifies that debt rather than using visual confidence as a substitute for evidence.
