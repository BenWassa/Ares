## Part IV — Process Synthesis

### Reading the synthesis

The explainer above is an **Ares synthesis of processes discussed by Dutton, Boyanowsky & Bond (2005)**. It is not a named model from the authors, and it is not the six-stage or eight-stage taxonomy previously used in this repository.

The source paper moves between social structure, target-group construction, official authorization and organization, and theories of perpetrator transition. Ares therefore presents these as **interacting domains rather than a ladder**. The relationships describe ways one domain can shape or reinforce another; they do not claim that every case follows a fixed sequence.

### What the synthesis does not claim

*   No domain is a necessary or sufficient cause of genocide, massacre, or political slaughter.
*   The four domains are not a severity scale, prediction score, or checklist for classifying a society.
*   Genocide and military massacre share some mechanisms in the paper, but their contexts and violence dynamics are not treated as interchangeable.
*   Individual participation is not automatic. Dutton et al. discuss variation among perpetrators and examples of refusal alongside situational influence.

### Explanation is not an early-warning instrument

The previous Part IV converted the legacy ladder into a checklist of early-warning indicators. That step went beyond what had been source-mapped. Ares 2.0 does not present a predictive warning checklist here. Prevention and intervention claims belong in Part V and require their own explicit sourcing rather than inheriting authority from a diagram.
