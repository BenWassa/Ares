# The Human Story of Extreme Mass Homicide

**Dutton, Boyanowsky & Bond (2005)**  
*Extreme Mass Homicide: From Military Massacre to Genocide*

*A source-conscious interactive digital synopsis*

Ares is a long-form digital publication that interprets Dutton, Boyanowsky & Bond's 2005 paper alongside eight historical case studies. It is designed for durable reading first: the argument, cases, glossary, process synthesis and sources remain available as ordinary HTML, while small browser enhancements improve orientation and definition lookup.

## Executive summary

Dutton, Boyanowsky & Bond compare genocide, political slaughter and military massacre in order to ask which social, political, organizational and psychological mechanisms can help explain extreme mass homicide. Their analysis resists a comforting separation between inherently monstrous perpetrators and everyone else; situational conditions, authority, group dynamics and individual variation all matter.

Ares uses the paper as its principal scholarly frame, then places eight historical cases beside that frame. The cases are not scores, ranks or interchangeable examples. Each retains its own context, chronology, testimony and aftermath, and legacy case-level claims that have not yet been source-traced are labelled as such rather than silently upgraded to verified status.

Part IV presents a **four-domain Ares synthesis** of processes discussed in Dutton et al. It is not a stage model authored by those researchers, not the older six-stage or eight-stage taxonomy previously used in this repository, and not an early-warning score. The domains are intentionally non-sequential and can overlap, recur and reinforce one another.

## A note before you begin

This publication discusses genocide, massacre, forced starvation, sexual violence and violence against children. The material is presented without gore, spectacle or dramatic interface effects. Witness material is treated as evidence: quotation and attribution should remain attached, and unresolved source tracing is made visible rather than hidden.

## How to use Ares

You can read continuously or enter at any major part from the contents. Part I establishes concepts and theory. Part II contains the eight historical cases. Part III compares patterns across cases but should not be read as proving a universal sequence. Part IV gives the source-mapped Ares process synthesis and its limits. Part V states what this release can and cannot responsibly infer about prevention. The glossary and source registry remain ordinary fragment-link targets even when JavaScript is unavailable.

Ares uses two editorial voices. Narrative and testimony stay close to human evidence and attribution; analytical sections carry synthesis and interpretation. Typography, rules and spacing distinguish them without turning testimony into decorative quotation content.
