# Photography — content shortlist for research

**Companion to `05-photography.md`.** That spec sets the rules (documents and places, not
bodies; rights enforced by schema). This document is the **research brief**: what to actually
go looking for, case by case.

> **Verify everything here before use.** Titles, dates, holding institutions and rights
> status below are starting points for research, not established facts. Several are from
> recall and need confirmation against the holding archive. Rights status in particular must
> be confirmed at source — never inferred from "it's old" or "it's on Wikipedia".

---

## The editorial idea: *the record was there*

Working through the eight cases, one pattern recurs strongly enough to become the spine of
the whole photographic programme.

In case after case, **someone documented it, warned about it, or reported it — and was
ignored, denied, or contradicted at the time.**

- A journalist filed the truth from Ukraine in 1933 and a more famous correspondent
  contradicted him in print.
- A soldier wrote to Congress in 1969 because the chain of command had buried it.
- A force commander sent a fax in January 1994 describing what was being prepared.
- The Security Council declared a town a safe area in 1993.
- Two reporters described a massacre in El Salvador in January 1982 and were publicly
  discredited by their own government.

This is not a decorative theme. It maps directly onto what the publication already argues
about **authorization, denial and contested record**, and it gives the imagery a job beyond
atmosphere. It also has the practical advantage of pointing almost entirely at **documents**,
which is where the rights position is cleanest.

**Proposed treatment:** one primary document per case, reproduced large enough to read, with
a caption that says what it is, who ignored it, and what happened next.

## Per-case candidates

Each entry lists **A** (documentary), **B** (place, present day), and where useful **C**
(period context / propaganda). Ranked — first listed is my recommendation.

### 1. Armenian Genocide (1915–17)

- **A1.** Ambassador Henry Morgenthau Sr.'s cables to the US State Department, 1915. *US
  federal record — likely public domain. Check NARA.*
- **A2.** *The Treatment of Armenians in the Ottoman Empire* (Bryce & Toynbee, HMSO, 1916) —
  the "Blue Book". Title page or a document page. *1916 UK government publication; check
  Crown copyright expiry.*
- **A3.** The Tehcir Law / Temporary Law of Deportation, 27 May 1915 — published Ottoman text.
- **B.** Tsitsernakaberd memorial complex, Yerevan. *CC-licensed images common on Commons.*
- **Note.** The Deir ez-Zor memorial was destroyed in 2014. A pre-2014 photograph, captioned
  as such, would say something true about how this history continues to be contested.

### 2. Ukrainian Holodomor (1932–33)

- **A1. The strongest pairing in the whole programme.** Gareth Jones's March 1933 press
  release and dispatches, set against Walter Duranty's *New York Times* copy denying famine.
  Two documents, same weeks, opposite claims. *Jones's papers are held in Wales
  (Aberystwyth); Duranty's NYT copy is under NYT copyright — likely quote as text rather
  than reproduce as image.*
- **A2.** The "Law of Spikelets" (7 August 1932) and the *chorni doshky* blacklist decrees.
  *Declassified Soviet decrees held in Ukrainian state archives.*
- **B.** Holodomor Genocide Museum, Kyiv.
- **Caution.** Famine photography of starving children is widely circulated, frequently
  misattributed (many "Holodomor" images are from the 1921–22 Volga famine), and depicts
  identifiable dying children. Rule out.

### 3. Nanking Massacre (1937)

- **A1.** John Rabe's diary, or the International Committee for the Nanking Safety Zone
  records. *Rabe's diary is published; underlying manuscript rights need checking.*
- **A2.** IMTFE (Tokyo Tribunal) judgment, 1948 — the Nanking findings. *International
  tribunal document; generally permissive.*
- **A3.** Minnie Vautrin's diary (Ginling College).
- **B.** Nanjing Massacre Memorial Hall.
- **Caution.** Nanking photography is both graphic and historically contested, with a long
  history of misattribution used in denial arguments. Using a contested image would hand a
  weapon to denial. Documents only.

### 4. My Lai Massacre (1968)

- **A1.** **Ron Ridenhour's letter, 29 March 1969**, sent to Congress and the Pentagon — the
  single document that forced the investigation. One page, written by a soldier who was not
  there and would not let it go. It is the case's hinge and it belongs to the individual-
  refusal thread the publication already draws. *Private letter; rights need checking, but
  widely reproduced in official records.*
- **A2.** The **Peers Inquiry** report (US Army, 1970) — title page plus a findings page on
  command failure and suppression. *US federal work, public domain.*
- **A3.** Hugh Thompson's testimony transcript — pairs directly with the quotation already in
  the case.
- **B.** Sơn Mỹ Memorial, Quảng Ngãi.
- **Rights note.** Ronald Haeberle shot two sets that day: **official Army frames (public
  domain)** and **personal colour frames (his copyright)**. Both are graphic. Recommend
  against either regardless of rights.

### 5. Cambodian Killing Fields (1975–79)

- **A1.** An S-21 (Tuol Sleng) **administrative record** — a prisoner log, a directive, an
  interrogation protocol. The bureaucratic paperwork of extermination is the analytic point:
  this was administered. *Held by DC-Cam (Documentation Center of Cambodia); licensing must
  be requested.*
- **A2.** ECCC (Extraordinary Chambers) judgment — Case 002 findings. *UN-backed court,
  permissive.*
- **B.** Tuol Sleng Genocide Museum buildings, or Choeung Ek.
- **Hard exclusion.** The S-21 **mugshots of prisoners** are among the most reproduced
  atrocity images in existence. They are portraits of identifiable people photographed
  immediately before their murder, by their murderers. Do not use them, under any licence.

### 6. El Mozote Massacre (1981)

- **A1. The second great pairing.** Raymond Bonner's *New York Times* and Alma
  Guillermoprieto's *Washington Post* dispatches (January 1982), set against the **US State
  Department cables and congressional certification denying the massacre**. The cables are
  *US federal records, public domain*, and were declassified. This is the denial mechanism
  documented in its own words.
- **A2.** *From Madness to Hope*, the UN Truth Commission for El Salvador report (1993). *UN
  document.*
- **A3.** Argentine Forensic Anthropology Team (EAAF) findings — the exhumation that
  established the child victim count. *Text/report, not remains imagery.*
- **B.** The El Mozote memorial wall listing the children's names.
- **Note.** El Mozote's chronology runs to 2016. It is the clearest case of the
  event/aftermath ratio that Figure 02 is designed to reveal — pair the imagery with that.

### 7. Rwandan Genocide (1994)

- **A1.** The **Dallaire fax**, 11 January 1994 — UNAMIR force commander to UN DPKO, warning
  of arms caches and prepared killing lists, three months before. One page. Enormous
  analytical weight, and it maps precisely onto the *authorization and organization* domain.
  *UN document; widely reproduced; confirm reuse terms.*
- **A2.** The "Hutu Ten Commandments" (*Kangura*, December 1990) and RTLM broadcast
  transcripts. **Must be captioned as propaganda at the point of use.** These are the best
  available evidence for the *target-group construction* domain in the entire corpus.
- **A3.** ICTR *Akayesu* judgment (1998) — the first conviction for genocide by an
  international court.
- **B.** Kigali Genocide Memorial.
- **Hard exclusion.** Nyamata and Ntarama churches display human remains and clothing of the
  dead in situ. Widely photographed, frequently freely licensed, and excluded by this
  programme's own rule.

### 8. Bosnia — Srebrenica (1992–95)

- **A1.** **UN Security Council Resolution 819** (16 April 1993) declaring Srebrenica a "safe
  area". Read against what happened in July 1995, the document is devastating without a word
  of editorialising. *UN document, permissive.*
- **A2.** ICTY *Krstić* judgment (2001) — the first legal finding of genocide at Srebrenica.
  *ICTY records are largely public.*
- **A3.** The NIOD report (2002) on Dutchbat, or Dutchbat's own situation reports.
- **B.** Srebrenica–Potočari Memorial Centre; the Battery Factory that served as the
  Dutchbat compound.
- **Hard exclusion.** The Scorpions execution video is evidence in law and spectacle on a
  website. Excluded.

## Practical research notes

- **Prioritise institutions with published reuse terms**: NARA, the UN Digital Library, ICTY
  and ICTR case records, the US Government Publishing Office, national archives with open
  licences. These resolve fastest.
- **Wikimedia Commons is a finding aid, not a rights source.** Licences there are frequently
  wrong on historical material. Always trace back to the holding institution.
- **"Public domain in the US" is not "public domain everywhere."** GitHub Pages serves
  globally. Where a work is PD in one jurisdiction only, record that in `licenceNote`.
- **Request permission where it is cheap to do so.** Memorial institutions and documentation
  centres (DC-Cam, the Kigali Memorial, the Srebrenica Memorial) frequently grant educational
  reuse on request. A short, specific email describing this project would likely succeed and
  is worth more than a legally grey Commons file.
- **Budget one clean document per case.** Eight excellent, readable, properly cleared
  documents would transform the publication. Twenty half-cleared images would sink it.

## What would be lost if this lane is cut

Ares would keep zero photographic content, but the four data-driven figures (02–04) plus the
process cycle (01) still deliver a complete visual-explanation layer. **This lane is the
highest-value and the highest-risk of the five, and it is the only one that can be deferred
indefinitely without leaving a hole in the argument.** Treat it as an enrichment programme
with no fixed deadline, not a release blocker.
